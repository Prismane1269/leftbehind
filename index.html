InSight · 2018–2022

Press play on a Marsquake

InSight put a seismometer straight onto Martian ground and felt 1,319 quakes in four years. The biggest, on Sol 1222, was about magnitude 5. This waveform is drawn to match the shape of that event: P-wave first, then the S-wave, then a slow surface rumble.

    07 · why they were left behind

    Nobody forgets these machines. They just cannot come home.

    “Discarded” sounds careless. It was the opposite — every one of these was left on purpose, for a reason an engineer could explain.

    • Coming home is expensive. Apollo could only fly the top half of its lander back, so the bottom half — engine, legs, gold foil — stayed on the Moon to save weight for the trip home.
    • Some finished the job. Opportunity was promised 90 days and drove for 14 years. When the dust storm finally won, there was nothing left to fix from Earth.
    • Some ran out of fuel, power or luck. Solar panels dust over. Batteries drain. A rotor breaks. A radio goes quiet mid-sentence.
    • Some are still working, which is the best outcome of all: Curiosity, Perseverance, and both Voyagers are still sending data today.

    The science did not stop

    Retroreflectors left by Apollo crews are still used tonight to measure the Moon's distance. InSight's quiet seismometer still hands scientists data about Mars's core. Ingenuity's flight log became the textbook for flying on other worlds.

    And we are going back

    Future missions are already planning to visit, photograph and learn from hardware that has spent decades in the dust. Some of it, like the samples Perseverance is dropping, is waiting to be collected.
